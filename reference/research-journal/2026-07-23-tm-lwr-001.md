# TM-LWR-001 bounded lowering contract — 2026-07-23

Paper hook: methods and reproducibility evidence for the bounded-lowering section of the
agentic-optimization evaluation paper; specifically, the penalty-certificate limits and the
anti-echo native-witness path.

## Scope

This entry records the `TM-LWR-001` handoff: one binary cubic Rosenberg reduction, finite
knapsack slack lowering, finite symmetric-TSP two-way-one-hot lowering, their direct
exhaustive evidence, and the benchmark bridge path. This is deliberately not a universal
quadratization library, automatic penalty tuner, constraint compiler, or sampler-quality
claim.

## Decisions and rejected alternatives

- The canonical QUBO/Ising offset is retained, including all squared-penalty constants. The
  rejected alternative was reporting only interaction energy, which would make encoded and
  native objective relations unauditable.
- The cubic catalog is one ordered three-variable binary monomial with one deterministic,
  typed product ancilla. A general higher-degree transform was rejected because it exceeds the
  task's truth-table proof and test envelope.
- Knapsack uses the evaluation fixture's lexicographic objective `-(C + 1) * value + weight`
  plus a bounded inequality slack representation. The rejected alternative was to rank a
  decoded selection by penalized QUBO energy; the bridge instead ranks feasible decoded
  selections by native value, then native weight.
- TSP uses an explicit symmetric distance matrix and two-way one-hot QUBO. The bridge ranks
  only decoded permutations by native cyclic tour length, rather than trusting the sampler's
  reported encoded energy.
- Strict `P` statuses are exact-arithmetic certificates. For derived constraint policies the
  default chooses an integer margin where representable. The rejected alternative was to
  describe `nextafter(boundary, +inf)` as a universal binary64 uniqueness guarantee.
- The cubic artifact declares a `1e-6` finite sensitivity margin for its observed above-bound
  uniqueness case. It separately retains the failed one-ULP observation rather than dropping
  it from the evidence.

## Mathematical and source record

`EVAL-EQ-025` in `reference/08-evaluation/equation-audit.md` was added before implementation.
It records the exact-arithmetic bounds: `P >= abs(c)` for cubic source-minimum preservation
and strict `P > abs(c)` for ancilla uniqueness; `P > max(0, max_i((C + 1) * value_i -
weight_i))` for the knapsack feasibility-ground-state certificate; and `P > B * U / 2` for
the TSP declared-feasible-tour certificate. Equality and below-bound counterexamples are
exhaustively retained.

Primary and official sources checked on 2026-07-23:

- Mandal et al., [Compressed Quadratization of Higher Order Binary Optimization Problems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00658), for Rosenberg reduction context.
- Lucas, [Ising formulations of many NP problems](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphy.2014.00005),
  sections 5.2 and 7.2, for standard knapsack/TSP formulation context.
- Ayodele, [Penalty Weights in QUBO Formulations: Permutation Problems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.11040), for permutation/one-hot penalty-weight context.
- D-Wave Ocean, [`dimod.make_quadratic`](https://docs.dwavequantum.com/en/latest/ocean/api_ref_dimod/generated/dimod.make_quadratic.html), for the documented warning that insufficient reduction strength can alter minimizers.
- Alessandroni et al., [Alleviating the quantum Big-M problem](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41534-025-01067-0), and [Scalable Determination of Penalization Weights for Constrained Optimizations on Approximate Solvers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02416), for penalty-selection limits and Gibbs-solver context.
- Doucet et al., [Thermodynamic significance of QUBO encoding on quantum annealers](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/ae6e98), for the practical relevance of encoding/penalty regimes.

Those sources motivate recording a policy and its limits. They do not replace the finite
ground-state proofs, prove a globally minimal penalty, or establish THRML mixing.

## Failures retained

- Red-first focused tests initially failed with `ModuleNotFoundError` before the new lowering
  modules existed. They then defined the public seams rather than serving as post-hoc tests.
- A temporary negative-cubic boundary test chose the wrong source word and failed with an
  energy-order assertion (`3.999997...` was not below `0`). The source word was corrected to
  the actual invalid-ancilla counterexample; the failure is not discarded from this record.
- The artifact initially treated `nextafter(abs(c), +inf)` as an observed uniqueness case. The
  one-ULP binary64 tie above falsified that gate. It is now a retained negative probe, and the
  positive artifact case uses the declared finite margin.
- Invoking the generator without `--overwrite` against the existing run directory raised the
  intended `FileExistsError`; overwrite now rejects unexpected entries and never recursively
  deletes the artifact directory.
